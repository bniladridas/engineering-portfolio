Over the past few years I've been thinking more about maintainability than velocity.

The longer I build software, the less I believe writing code is the difficult part. Keeping software understandable, maintainable, and adaptable is.

I wrote a short article, *Building Software That Lasts*, reflecting on lessons from building projects and contributing to open source.

Three things I keep coming back to:

1. Boring, predictable code beats clever code. Every time.
2. Interfaces outlast implementations. Spend your design energy on the boundary.
3. Small decisions compound — good ones and bad ones alike.

I hope it's useful to others who enjoy building systems that evolve over time.
